# Round 12: surfaces (watch voice, timeline media, approval verbs)

> ⌚ *"Hey Muse, I'm running 15 late. Tell office hours on Slack, push my 3 PM block, and draft a reply to Prof. Lee."* (GP1, walking)

GP1 starts on the wrist, but a real Apple Watch can't keep the `/v1/voice` socket open. So the watch now records while you hold, signs the clip with its Secure Enclave key, and uploads it in one request. If Core can't be reached from the watch, the iPhone carries the signed clip unchanged. The iPhone timeline also shows pictures now: the crop of what the camera saw, and a live thumbnail of the browser during computer use. And the approve button now says what it does: **Post**, **Invite** or **Confirm**.

## What's built
### 1. Watch voice: `POST /v1/voice/clip`
**Core.**
| File | What it does |
|---|---|
| `voice/clip.ts` (new) | **`acceptClip`.** Body: PCM16 mono at 24 kHz, at most 30 s (1,440,000 bytes, the same cap as a streamed turn). **A paired device** sends `x-auren-device`, `x-auren-timestamp` (ms since 1970) and `x-auren-signature` over `auren-clip\n<device id>\n<timestamp>\n<sha256 hex of body>`. Core checks the device is paired and not revoked, then the signature, then **freshness** (±60 s of Core's clock), then the **replay cache**. Only a valid signature reaches the cache, so a stranger can't fill it. **This Mac** (`isLocal`) may post unsigned with `x-auren-surface`. Anything else from the network gets 403. A bad signature, a late clip or a repeated clip is logged as an `error` event, because a relayed clip has nobody waiting for the reply. Returns `200 { text }`, where `text` is the command it ran, or `""` if it heard only the wake phrase. Other statuses: 400 (empty or odd-length audio, bad timestamp), 413, 502/503 (transcriber). **`ReplayCache`** keeps each taken clip until it would be stale anyway, holds at most 10,000, and fails closed when full. |
| `voice/gateway.ts` | **Reused, not forked.** The socket's turn ending (`stripWake`, the metadata-only `voice.final` ops line, the ledger row, `onCommand`) moves into one `settleTurn`, which both paths call. **`transcribeClip`** opens the same `AsrConnector` and sends the clip in 100 ms frames (4,800 bytes). It waits for a final after `end`, or acts on the last partial after the same 5 s grace. `MAX_TURN_BYTES` is exported so both caps are the same number. |
| `security/devices.ts` | `clipPayload(deviceId, timestamp, pcm)`, next to the other payloads |
| `server.ts` | One marked `R12` block after the crops route: `bodyLimit` (413 before the body is read) and the route |

**AurenKit.**
| File | What it does |
|---|---|
| `Clip.swift` (new) | `DeviceIdentity.clipPayload/signClip` (same bytes as Core) · `ClipBuffer` (thread-safe; stops at 30 s) · **`VoiceClip`** (`make` signs; `request(core:token:)` builds the POST; `metadata` gives the headers for WatchConnectivity; `init(pcm:metadata:)` rebuilds the clip on the iPhone) · **`ClipSender`**, the watch's side: it posts directly, and only on a `URLError` (Core unreachable) hands the clip to the relay. A refusal from Core is shown, not relayed. · **`ClipRelayHost`**, the iPhone's side: it posts the same bytes and headers with its own address and token |
| `ClipRecorder.swift` (new, watchOS) | Hold to talk: `AVAudioEngine` input tap, each 100 ms buffer converted by the existing `PCMConverter` (it already built for watchOS), auto-stop at 30 s, under 0.25 s ignored as a tap, `onSend` for the haptic; `phase` is `idle · recording · sending · sent(text) · relayed · failed` |
| `WatchLink.swift` | watchOS: `transferClip` writes the clip to a temp file and calls `transferFile` with the headers as metadata; the file is removed when the transfer finishes. iOS: `onClip` reads the received file (refusing over 30 s of audio) before WatchConnectivity deletes it. |

**Apps.** The watch has a hold-to-talk mic button (one clip per press), a status line ("Listening…", the words Core heard, "Sent through your iPhone", or why it stopped), a `.click` haptic as the clip leaves, and the existing `.notification` haptic when an approval arrives. `project.yml` adds the watch's `NSMicrophoneUsageDescription`. The iPhone app sets `WatchLink.onClip` to `ClipRelayHost.forward`.

### 2. Timeline media on the iPhone
- **`browser_frame`** decodes in AurenKit (`EventKind.browserFrame`; it was `.unknown`). `BrowserFrame(event:)` accepts only a `data:image/jpeg;base64,` URL whose bytes start `FF D8 FF`, at most 200 KB (the driver hub's cap). The length is checked before decoding. `EventStyle` gives it "Browser" (or "Stopped") and the console's line: `www.opentable.com · step 3 · waiting for your tap`. The iPhone shows the **newest** frame as a thumbnail (`liveBrowserFrameID`); older steps stay one line each, so 25 steps don't fill the screen with pictures.
- **Crops.** `AurenEvent.cropID` accepts only `crop_` plus 16 lowercase hex digits (CropStore's format), so nothing else can become part of the URL. `CropImage.request` builds `GET /v1/crops/:id` with the bearer token. `CropImage.fetch` takes only a 200 of JPEG bytes under 1 MB. The receipt row shows the crop at the console's size.
- **How a device authenticates the crop GET: with the token it already holds, and no new signed scheme.** `/v1/events` returns the whole timeline (every summary, and the crop ids) behind the bearer token alone, and the app already sends that token for it. A device signature on the crop would guard a picture less sensitive than the text beside it, while that text stays token-only. It would also need a nonce or timestamp scheme for GETs. If Core ever puts `/v1/events` behind device signatures, crops should follow.
- The watch shows neither picture: its screen is for approvals.

### 3. Approval verbs (Surface Protocol v0.1)
- `ToolSpec.verb?: string`, set on the demo world's `calendar.invite` ("Invite") and `slack.post_message` ("Post"), on the live Slack `post_message` ("Post") and Google Calendar `invite` ("Invite"), and on `browser.run_task` ("Confirm", used at the commit point). **Gmail has no send-tier tool** (Auren only drafts), so nothing says "Send" yet.
- `SurfaceRegistry.requestApproval(summary, tier, timeoutMs, verb?)` keeps the verb with the pending approval. `approval_request` carries `verb` only when there is one, and it travels with a handoff. `executeCall` passes `tool.verb` in its two existing `requestApproval` calls and is otherwise unchanged.
- The console, the iPhone and the watch show it on the gold button (`ApprovalRequest.actionLabel`; trimmed, at most 24 characters, "Approve" when missing).
- **The signed payload is unchanged.** It is still `auren-approve\n<request>\n<1|0>\n<sha256(summary)>`. The verb is display only, and Core derives it from the tool spec, never from the model or a page. The spec says so.

## How to run it
```bash
cd core && node --test src/voice/clip.test.ts          # the clip route, no key
cd apps/apple/AurenKit && swift test --filter "Clip|TimelineMedia|ApprovalVerb"
make apple-build                                        # both apps, Simulator, no signing
```
Try a clip against `make dev` from this Mac, unsigned (it needs `MODEL_API_KEY` for the transcriber):
```bash
sox -d -r 24000 -c 1 -b 16 -e signed -t raw - trim 0 3 | \
  curl -s --data-binary @- -H 'x-auren-surface: web-1' http://127.0.0.1:8787/v1/voice/clip
```
On devices, pair the watch, then hold the mic button and let go: the words Core heard appear under it. When the watch can't reach Core's address itself (Wi-Fi off, the iPhone nearby), the relay takes over and the watch says "Sent through your iPhone".

What the Core tests cover, all over real HTTP with a scripted transcriber:
- a paired watch's signed clip is accepted (200), arrives as 12 frames of 4,800 bytes, and runs as an `utterance` from that watch with the wake phrase removed;
- a bad signature is 403: over other bytes, or by another key, and logged;
- a replay is 403 and makes no second command;
- a stale clip, or one from the future, is 403 and logged;
- a clip over 30 s is 413, answered from `content-length`;
- not paired, revoked, or unsigned from the network is 403;
- wake phrase only gives `200 { text: "" }` and no command;
- this Mac may post unsigned, and empty or odd-length audio is 400;
- with no transcriber, 503;
- replay-cache expiry;
- `clipPayload` pinned to a literal that **AurenKit's test pins too**, plus a **CryptoKit-made clip signature** (P256.Signing, DER) that verifies in Node, and fails with the timestamp off by 1 ms;
- the verb on `approval_request`, absent when unset (v0 shape) and kept across a handoff; the Slack post's request says "Post" through the real agent loop; and a signed approval with a verb verifies with the unchanged payload.

The AurenKit tests cover:
- the clip payload matches Core byte for byte;
- the request builder sets the method, body, content type, token and the device, surface and timestamp headers, and its signature verifies only over the exact bytes and timestamp;
- unsigned clips name only the surface;
- what Core would refuse, `VoiceClip.make` refuses too;
- `ClipBuffer` stops at 30 s;
- **the relay hands the exact bytes through**: watch → in-memory file link → `ClipRelayHost`, with the same body and the same six headers as a direct post, and the signature still verifies;
- a 403 from Core is shown, not relayed;
- `browser_frame` decoding and its style and line;
- PNG, http, non-JPEG and bad-base64 frames, and frames over 200 KB, are refused;
- the newest-frame pick;
- the crop request carries the token, and bad crop ids (`../trust`, uppercase, the wrong length, `/`) never make a request;
- a crop fetch refuses a 404, HTML and an oversized body;
- the verb decodes, falls back to "Approve", and is not in the signed bytes.

## What isn't verified
- **No real Apple Watch:**
  - `AVAudioEngine` input on watchOS, the microphone permission prompt, and the format the watch mic delivers (the converter is tested from 48 kHz float on the Mac);
  - recording while walking;
  - how the haptics feel;
  - the watch's clock against the Mac's (the 60 s window).
- **No real iPhone:**
  - WatchConnectivity `transferFile` delivery, and how fast it is;
  - whether iOS wakes the iPhone app in the background to receive the file, and gives it enough time to post within 60 s (there's no background-task request around the post);
  - the crop and frame thumbnails on a screen.
- **Watch networking:** whether the watch reaches Core's LAN address directly (through the iPhone's proxy or Wi-Fi), and how long a failed attempt takes before the relay. The request's idle timeout is 12 s.
- **No live ASR:** a whole clip is sent to Muse Voice Transcribe in 100 ms frames as fast as the socket takes them, not in real time. Whether the realtime endpoint accepts burst audio, and how fast it is, is untested. If it needs pacing, change only the loop in `transcribeClip`.
- **No UI was looked at.** The talk button, status line, thumbnails and verb buttons are compiled, not seen in the Simulator. The console's verb button wasn't opened in a browser.

## Known limits
- Clips carry no server nonce, because the relay can't fetch one. A clip that's held back but never delivered could still be posted by whoever holds it, within 60 s of recording. Replays are refused.
- The replay cache lives in memory. A restart of Core forgets it, but the 60 s window still bounds a replay to just after a restart.
- A relayed clip's answer isn't sent back to the watch. The watch says "Sent through your iPhone", and the words show on the timeline as the `utterance`.
- The reply speaks on the watch only if its surface socket (direct or relayed) is online. Otherwise Core routes it to the next voice surface, as for any command.
- The computer-use safety checks (`approveCheck` in `browser.ts`) still say "Approve"; only the commit says "Confirm".
- The crop is fetched once per row view, with no cache beyond the row. Without `AUREN_TOKEN`, Core binds to 127.0.0.1, so device clips and crops work only from the Simulator.
